A HISTORY OF CONNECTION

Subtitle placeholder — swap in final copy.

1450s

PRESS

Ideas can reach beyond their original audience.

Before the printing press, written ideas were harder to reproduce at scale. The press made it possible for information, stories, and culture to circulate far beyond their original audience.

1920s

RADIO

The masses can experience the same moment together.

Before radio, mass communication was experienced through print or live performance. Radio created a new kind of connection by allowing the masses to experience the same moment together.

1950s

TELEVISION

Visual language became part of daily life.

Before television, mass communication relied on words and sound. Television added a shared visual language. The screen became part of daily life, shaping the conversations that defined culture.

1990s

INTERNET

Connection expanded beyond our immediate world.

Before the internet, access to culture was shaped by the world around you. The internet connected us to information and people beyond our immediate reach.

2000s

SOCIAL MEDIA

Personal expression became public identity.

Before social media, the internet felt like a place to explore and connect. Social media turned personal expression into public identity and audience building.

2020s

ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE

Technology can now take part in the creative process.

Before AI, technology helped people access, create, and share entertainment. Now that AI can take on more of the creative process, who gets to shape what comes next?

DECENTRALIZED CULTURE

The future of entertainment should not be shaped by a single gatekeeper. Decentralized culture means building creator-owned infrastructure that serves the people who create the value. We do not have to give up our independence to build something together.
